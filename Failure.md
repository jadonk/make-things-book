## A Diagnosis of Failure

Catastrophic failures have often exhibited a common set of fundamental causes. From the Tacoma bridge collapse, the Challenger explosion, the Deepwater Horizon blowout, Boeing's 737 MAX crashes, and the 2003 North American Blackout, to a 50-foot-tall steel molassas storage tank flooding Boston in 1919, the same two driving causes can be observed over and over again: tight coupling and drift.

Tight coupling is when a complex system's proper function depends on elements that are highly connected and interdependent. When failure in one element has a major impact on the rest of the system, you can be confident that trouble is brewing.

Drift is when the common forces of competition, scarcity and production pressure are allowed to gradually increase tolerances beyond an acceptable limit. This is called drift because it often happens slowly in significant disasters, because success can be a false indicator of acceptable risk.

These same causes can be observed in failures with somewhat less obviously harmful effects. Digital platforms are particularly vulnerable to designs that lock users into a complete system from a single vendor creating a tight coupling and twiddling the system under market pressures to drift into failure. I'll use Amazon as an example of a digital platform that has generated a tight coupling and has demonstrated drift into an abusive relationship with its employees, suppliers and customers.

## Pedagogical Approaches to Ethical Making

### Engaging Experts

Students are likely to engage communities of experts and non-experts alike, often without enough background to identify the best place to ask their questions or the difference between a good answer and a bad one. Without experience engaging busy experts, they might also not pose the question[^16] in a way that is most likely to generate a useful response.

Here's my list of questions that should be answered within any question to experts:

1) What are you trying to acomplish?
2) What research did you do to identify an approach?
3) What did you actually try?
4) What was the result and why did it surprise you?
5) What would you consider success?
